Legal

Copyright and takedown policy

Last updated 28 October 2026. This explains who owns what, how to report material you believe infringes your copyright, and how to dispute a report made in error. It is written in plain language. It is not legal advice and it does not decide whether any particular file may lawfully be distributed.

1. Scope

Autopatcher is an unofficial open-source project, published at github.com/pxdns/autopatcherbuild, with this website. This policy covers the repository, its releases and this site. It does not cover third-party servers, packs or mods hosted elsewhere, the other repositories Autopatcher builds on, or Minecraft itself.

We do not host uploads from the public. We still follow the principles of the United States Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA) so that rights holders have a clear way to reach us. Following that process does not mean we claim, or have been found to have, any legal protection under that law.

3. What this project hosts

A release contains the patcher program and a patch bundle. The bundle is built to hold no Minecraft code: new files written for this project, plus changes made only of added lines, with removed original lines recorded as hashes. Every build runs a leak scan that fails if the bundle contains a run of lines copied from Minecraft's decompiled code. The official game files are downloaded by you, from Mojang, onto your own computer. If you believe a release still contains your material, use the process below.

4. How to file a copyright notice

  1. Confirm you can actYou must own the right, be an exclusive licensee, or be authorised in writing by the owner. Keep the proof: a registration, a licence or assignment, dated source files, or a signed letter of authority.
  2. Check the use is not authorisedLook at the licence files and the credits. A use that follows an open-source licence you granted is not infringement. Consider whether the use might be a fair use or other permitted exception before sending a notice; a notice sent without that thought can be challenged as a misrepresentation (section 7).
  3. Identify exactly what you object toGive the file path or page address and a permalink to the commit or release asset, and say which of your works it copies. Vague requests cannot be acted on and will be returned.
  4. Gather evidenceLink the original work, add a side-by-side comparison or file hashes showing the copy, and your proof from step 1. Do not send passwords, keys or private data.
  5. Send itTo us: open an issue titled “Copyright notice” on the issue tracker, or contact the maintainer privately through the GitHub profile pxdns if the notice has personal details. To the host: use GitHub's own DMCA form. You may send it to both.
  6. Write the noticeInclude: your full legal name, address and a way to reach you, with your signature (typed is fine); the work you say was infringed; the exact locations of the infringing material; a statement that you believe in good faith that the use is not authorised by you, your agent or the law; and a statement, under penalty of perjury, that the notice is accurate and that you are the owner or authorised to act.

5. What we do when we receive a notice

  • We read it and, if it is complete, remove or disable the material identified, or tell you why we cannot. If it is incomplete we ask for what is missing.
  • We tell the person responsible for the material, where we can, and give them a copy of the notice with your contact details removed where the law allows.
  • If material is removed in error, we restore it as soon as the question is settled.

6. How to dispute a report (counter-notice)

If material of yours was removed and you believe that was a mistake or a misidentification, send us a counter-notice in the same way: your name, address and contact details; the material and where it was before removal; a statement under penalty of perjury that you believe in good faith it was removed by mistake; and your agreement to the jurisdiction that applies to you. We may restore the material unless the rights holder tells us they have started legal action.

7. False or abusive reports

Knowingly sending a false notice or counter-notice can have legal consequences. We may ignore reports that are bulk, automated or plainly unrelated to this project.

8. Repeat infringement

We may remove contributors' access or stop distributing material from anyone who repeatedly infringes.

9. Notices from Mojang or Microsoft

If Mojang Studios or Microsoft tell us that something here does not meet their terms, we take it seriously and act on it promptly. Use the same route as above.
